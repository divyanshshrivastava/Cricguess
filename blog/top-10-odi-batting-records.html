Top 10 Unbreakable ODI Batting Records That Defy Belief

Published: October 2026 • 6 min read • By CricGuess Editorial Team

One Day International (ODI) cricket has witnessed dramatic tactical revolutions over the past five decades. From the cautious 60-over innings of the 1970s to modern batting masterclasses where 350-plus totals are regularly chased down, individual milestones have constantly redefined what was once deemed humanly impossible.

Here, we analyze ten legendary ODI batting feats that statistical experts believe may stand uncontested for generations to come.

1. Rohit Sharma's Colossal 264 vs Sri Lanka (2014)

On November 13, 2014, at the historic Eden Gardens in Kolkata, Indian opening batter Rohit Sharma scripted an innings that felt like a video game on easy mode. Blasting 33 fours and 9 sixes off 173 deliveries, Rohit posted 264 — a score higher than what many full national teams muster across 50 overs.

With Rohit also owning double centuries against Australia (209) and Sri Lanka again (208*), his status as the only human with three ODI double tons is safe for decades.

2. Sachin Tendulkar's Monument of 18,426 Career Runs

Over a majestic 23-year career spanning 463 matches, India's legendary "Master Blaster" amassed an astronomical 18,426 ODI runs with 49 centuries and 96 half-centuries. With modern cricketers managing heavy multi-format workloads and rotating out of bilateral series, reaching 18,000 runs requires unyielding fitness, consistency, and two full decades of international presence.

3. AB de Villiers' Lightning 31-Ball Century (2015)

On January 18, 2015, at the Wanderers Stadium in Johannesburg, South Africa's AB de Villiers put on a batting exhibition against the West Indies that defied geometry. Walking in at the 39th over, Mr. 360 reached his half-century in just 16 deliveries, before thundering to the fastest century in ODI history in just 31 balls!

4. Virat Kohli's 50 ODI Centuries

At the 2023 ICC Men's Cricket World Cup semi-final at the Wankhede Stadium, Virat Kohli surpassed Sachin Tendulkar's long-standing tally by hitting his 50th ODI century. Doing so in just under 280 innings highlighted a conversion rate unmatched by any batter in cricket history.

5. Sourav Ganguly & Rahul Dravid's 318-Run Partnership (1999)

During the 1999 World Cup in Taunton, India lost an early wicket against Sri Lanka before Sourav Ganguly (183) and Rahul Dravid (145) forged a mammoth 318-run partnership for the second wicket — the first-ever 300-run stand in ODI cricket.
